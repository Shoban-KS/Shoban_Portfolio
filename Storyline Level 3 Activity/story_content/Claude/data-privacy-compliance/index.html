Foundations

What Is Personal Data?

0:00 / 0:00

Transcript: Let's look at what counts as personal data. Direct identifiers point to a specific person on their own - things like a full name, email address, government ID, or phone number. Indirect identifiers don't identify someone by themselves, but can when combined with other details, such as a job title, date of birth, or IP address. Sensitive data requires extra protection because exposure could cause real harm - health information, racial or ethnic origin, financial details, or biometric data. You handle this kind of information more often than you might realize.

  • Full name

Indirect identifiers don't identify someone by themselves, but can when combined with other details.

  • Job title + department

  • Health or medical information

  • Biometric data

  • A customer's name and order history in a support ticket

Core Principles

0:00 / 0:00

Transcript: Here are the core principles of data privacy. Data minimization means collecting only the data you actually need for the task. Purpose limitation means using data only for the reason it was originally collected. Consent means obtaining clear permission before collecting or using someone's data. Your Role

Employee Responsibilities

0:00 / 0:00

Transcript: Personal data moves through a lifecycle, and you have responsibilities at every stage. When collecting data, gather only what's needed for the task, and don't collect extra details just in case. When storing data, save it only in approved, secured systems, and lock your screen when you step away. When sharing data, double-check recipients before sending, and never use unapproved channels like personal email. And when disposing of data, delete it through approved retention processes once it's no longer needed, rather than letting old records sit indefinitely.

Personal data moves through a lifecycle. Use the arrows to move through each stage.

1. Collecting Data

Do: Collect only what's needed for the task at hand. Ask why a piece of data is required before requesting it.

Don't: Gather extra personal details "just in case" they're useful later.

2. Storing Data

Do: Save personal data only in approved, secured systems. Lock your screen when stepping away.

Don't: Save spreadsheets of customer data to your personal desktop or drive.

3. Sharing Data

Do: Double-check recipients before sending. Share only with people who need it for their role.

Don't: Forward personal data over unapproved channels or personal email.

4. Disposing of Data

Do: Delete data through approved retention/deletion processes once it's no longer needed.

Don't: Leave old records sitting indefinitely "just in case."
